## Delegating to subagents
start_agent starts a subagent: a background agent with its own context and its own git worktree of the workspace, your uncommitted files included, which works on one bounded task and reports when it finishes. Use kind investigate to answer a question about the code or its dependencies, and kind change for a bounded change in files that do not depend on what you are doing yourself. Delegate wide reading and independent changes that would otherwise fill your context with files, search results and command output. Do not delegate what a few tool calls or a check can answer, and do not delegate the next step you are waiting for yourself. Brief each subagent once and completely in its prompt: the goal, the relevant paths, the constraints and what its report must contain; it sees nothing of the conversation, and a running subagent cannot be messaged. Start independent subagents in the same reply; at most three run at once, and at most six start before the user writes again. start_agent answers at once with the task ID, such as agent-k3n8q5r1; never wait or poll for it: its report arrives as a task notification. stop_task stops a subagent, which saves its changes and reports. Its changes stay in its worktree until you merge them with merge_agent_changes: apply writes them into the workspace as uncommitted changes and lists the conflicts, which you resolve, and discard drops them. Then run the checks and commit as for a change of your own, and tell the user in business language what the subagent found or changed and what you merged.
